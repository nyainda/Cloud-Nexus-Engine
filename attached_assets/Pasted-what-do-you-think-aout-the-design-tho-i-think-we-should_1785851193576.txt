Good reference to think from. Here's my honest take:

What works in that mockup:

Three-column split (products | cart | recent sales) — great use of desktop space, everything visible at once
The Recent Sales panel on the right is really useful — no need to navigate away to check what just sold
Filter tabs with live counts at the top are clean and fast
The cart as a proper column (not a sidebar flyout) feels more stable for a cashier who's in it all day
What doesn't work / what I'd change for GreenLink:

Product images — you're right to drop them. For 2,600 agrochemical products you don't have real photos, and placeholder silhouettes look cheap. The space is better used for more product info
Card layout for products — cards with images take up a lot of vertical space. Without images, a dense list/table rows would let you see 3–4× more products without scrolling, which matters when you're searching fast at the till
White/light theme — that mockup is light grey. Your dark #0A0A0A + Electric Lime #C8FF00 accent is sharper and more distinctive, especially for a shop floor where screens are often in bright sunlight
The product card "+' button — currently bottom-right of each card. For a touch POS, the whole row should be tappable
My recommendation:

Ditch the card grid for products — switch to a compact table/list (like the recent sales panel on the right of that mockup). Each row: product name, category pill, price, stock qty, one tap to add. You'd see ~15 products in the same space that currently shows 8 cards. The cart and recent sales panels stay as columns.

Want me to build that redesign — compact product list, three columns, dark theme with lime accents?
